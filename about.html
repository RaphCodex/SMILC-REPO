Our Story

The Students’ Media and Information Literacy Club (SMILC) is a youth-led initiative dedicated to equipping students and young people with the knowledge, skills, and values needed for ethical and responsible engagement with media and information platforms.

In 2019 at the University of Ibadan, Nigeria, SMILC emerged from a shared vision between its co-founders, Josiah and Korede, who were inspired while taking the course POS 353: Media and the State, taught by Professor Atuiaje Irene Pogoson in the Department of Political Science.

Motivated by the power of media to shape societies, they committed to helping young people navigate today’s complex information landscape with integrity and discernment. The first university chapter of SMILC started in December 2021 at the University of Ibadan.

Our Mission & Vision

Our Mission

To equip students and young people with the knowledge, skills, and values needed for ethical, responsible, and informed use of media through training, advocacy, and capacity-building initiatives.

Our Vision

To promote a generation of ethically conscious and media-literate citizens who engage constructively with information and communication platforms to drive positive societal change.

Our Core Values

Integrity

Upholding truth and transparency in all media-related engagements.

Crtical Thinking

Encouraging independent, informed, and reflective judgment.

Collabration

Building partnerships for greater impact.

Civic Engagement

Promoting active participation in democratic and community life

Innovation

Ensuring equal access to media literacy opportunities for all.

Collaboration

Adopting creative approaches to teaching and applying media literacy.

What We Do

We provide hands-on training, host workshops, and run advocacy campaigns to promote Media and Information Literacy (MIL) in schools, universities, and the broader community.

Our initiatives include capacity-building programs, interactive discussions, and collaborative projects with local and international partners. As a proud member of UNESCO’s Media and Information Literacy Alliance, SMILC contributes to global efforts in building informed, resilient, and empowered societies.

Our Impact

Since its inception, SMILC has reached hundreds of students and young people through training programs, seminars, and community engagement projects. By equipping participants with tools to identify misinformation, analyze media messages, and use media ethically, we are cultivating informed citizens who are ready to lead change in their communities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the SMILC all about? ▼

Students' Media and Information Literacy Club (SMILC) is a student-led initiative focused on equipping young people with the skills to critically engage with media, identify misinformation, and responsibly use information technologies. Rooted in the principles of UNESCO’s Media and Information Literacy (MIL) framework, the club promotes digital responsibility, ethical communication, and informed civic participation.

Is SMILC affiliated with UNESCO? ▼

Yes. SMILC aligns with UNESCO's global Media and Information Literacy initiatives, and our programs are designed around UNESCO’s five MIL competencies. While not a direct subsidiary of UNESCO, we collaborate with UNESCO-inspired networks and frameworks to promote media literacy, especially among students and young adults.

Who can join SMILC, and what are the benefits of membership? ▼

Any student or young adult passionate about media literacy, digital safety, critical thinking, or civic education can join SMILC. Members gain access to exclusive trainings, workshops, outreach opportunities, and national/international MIL events. It’s also a platform to amplify your voice on digital issues affecting your community.

What kind of programs or activities does SMILC run? ▼

SMILC organizes periodic outreaches, campus campaigns, interactive colloquia, and online awareness drives. These events cover topics such as combating fake news, digital well-being, ethical online behavior, and evaluating online sources—reaching nearly a thousand young people in just over four years.

How can partnership with SMILC be achieved? ▼

We welcome partnerships with educational institutions, civil society groups, and government agencies to promote media and information literacy. Interested parties can reach out via our official social media platforms or email to initiate collaboration on tailored MIL programs or co-hosted events..
